If you've noticed a trail of ants marching across your kitchen counter in the summer, you're not alone. Ants are one of the most common summer pests — and one of the most frustrating, because the ants you see are only a tiny fraction of the colony.

## Why Ants Come Inside in Summer

When it's hot and dry outside, ants come indoors hunting for two things: **food and moisture**. Your kitchen and bathroom offer both. Sugary spills, crumbs, pet food, and even a leaky pipe are all it takes to attract a scout — and once one ant finds food, it lays a chemical trail for hundreds more to follow.

## Why Store-Bought Sprays Often Make It Worse

Here's the counterintuitive part: spraying the ants you see can actually **backfire**. With some species, spraying causes the colony to "bud" — splitting into multiple new colonies. You end up with more ant trails, not fewer.

The ants on your counter are foragers. The real target is the **queen and the colony**, which are usually hidden in a wall void, under the foundation, or out in the yard.

## What Actually Works

- **Baiting.** Slow-acting baits are carried back to the nest, wiping out the colony at the source.
- **Sanitation.** Wipe up spills, store food in sealed containers, and fix moisture problems.
- **Exclusion.** Seal the cracks and gaps ants use to get inside.
- **Species ID.** Different ants need different approaches — carpenter ants, for example, can damage wood and require targeted treatment.

## When to Call a Pro

If baiting and cleanup aren't cutting it, or you suspect carpenter ants, it's time for professional help. A licensed technician can identify the species, locate the colony, and treat it directly.
