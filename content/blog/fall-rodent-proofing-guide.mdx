Every fall, as the weather cools, mice and rats start searching for a warm, sheltered place to spend the winter. Unfortunately, your home is the perfect candidate. The good news: a little rodent-proofing now can save you a full-blown infestation later.

## How Rodents Get In

Mice can squeeze through a gap the size of a **dime**, and rats through one the size of a **quarter**. Common entry points include:

- Gaps around utility lines, pipes, and vents
- Damaged weatherstripping under garage and exterior doors
- Cracks in the foundation
- Openings where the roofline meets the walls

## Signs You Already Have a Problem

- Droppings in cabinets, drawers, or along walls
- Gnaw marks on food packaging or wiring
- Scratching or scurrying sounds in walls and ceilings at night
- A musty odor in enclosed spaces

## Your Fall Rodent-Proofing Checklist

1. **Seal entry points** with steel wool and caulk — rodents can't chew through steel wool.
2. **Store food** (including pet food and birdseed) in airtight containers.
3. **Declutter** garages, attics, and basements to remove nesting sites.
4. **Trim branches** away from the roofline so rodents can't climb in.
5. **Keep firewood** stacked away from the house.
6. **Fix moisture issues** — rodents need water, too.

## Why Exclusion Matters More Than Traps

Traps deal with the rodents already inside, but they don't stop new ones from following the same path in. Lasting control comes from **exclusion** — finding and sealing every entry point. That's exactly where a professional inspection pays off.
